Place first, then answer

In Health Promotion, age and life stage change the answer.

A question is often answerable once you know how old the client is and what stage of life they are in. The fastest route is to place the scenario before you reach for an action.

This resource helps you build that placing habit, then maps the high-yield priorities from prenatal care through older adulthood.

The placing move

Place the scenario in three steps

1

Before anything else, place the client in the correct stage of life.
2

Think immunizations, screenings, expected milestones, and anticipatory guidance.
3
Match the nursing action
Choose the action that fits the stage and its current promotion priorities.
Two modeled placings

See the placing move in action

Scenario: A parent brings their six-month-old infant to a well-child visit and asks the nurse what to expect at this stage.
Place the stage: This is infancy.
Recall the focus: CDC infant immunizations, safe sleep on the back in an empty crib, feeding guidance, and milestones such as sitting with support and babbling.
Match the action: Give the immunizations that are due and reinforce stage-appropriate anticipatory guidance on safe sleep, feeding, and injury prevention. The U.S. CDC schedule is the expected standard.
Scenario: An older adult client attends a routine wellness visit.
Place the stage: This is older adulthood.
Recall the focus: Zoster and pneumococcal immunizations, fall risk assessment, age-appropriate screening, and a review for polypharmacy.
Match the action: Provide due immunizations, assess fall risk, complete appropriate screening, and review the medication list for polypharmacy.

YOUR MENTAL MAP
Health Promotion changes with age: the immunization, screening, teaching, and milestone that matter for an infant are not the same ones that matter for an adolescent or older adult.
