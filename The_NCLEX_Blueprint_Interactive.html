Subtopic 2 Resource
The NCLEX Blueprint
Explore the four major NCLEX-RN Client Needs categories, their eight content areas, exam weights, competencies, and practical exam tips.
How to use this resource

Select any major category to expand it. Each section shows what the exam tests, the key competency areas within it, and a practical tip for approaching related questions.

Safe and Effective Care Environment2 subcategories · 31% combined
+

This category has two subcategories and together they represent the single largest portion of the exam.

Subcategory

Management of Care

18%
What this tests

How you coordinate, organize, delegate, prioritize, and make legally and ethically grounded decisions in a U.S. healthcare setting.

Key competency areas

Assignment, delegation and supervision; client rights; advocacy; informed consent; confidentiality and information security; legal rights and responsibilities; ethical practice; case management; continuity of care; multidisciplinary collaboration; performance improvement; establishing priorities.

Exam tip

When a question involves delegation, look for verbs like evaluate, assess, determine, teach, instruct, and decide. These belong to the RN and cannot be delegated. Admission assessment and discharge teaching also remain with the RN.

Subcategory

Safety and Infection Prevention and Control

13%
What this tests

The systems and behaviors that prevent patient and staff harm.

Key competency areas

Accident, error, and injury prevention; standard and transmission-based precautions; safe use of equipment and technology; emergency response and security plans; restraint use; reporting unsafe practice.

Exam tip

When a safety concern appears, the correct action is usually to act first and document second. Correct client identification, near-miss reporting, and escalation of unsafe practices are recurring priorities.

Major category

Health Promotion and Maintenance

9%
What this tests

Nursing care across the full lifespan, with a focus on prevention, early detection, and health education.

Key competency areas

Developmental stages across the lifespan; prenatal, perinatal, and postnatal care; pediatric growth and development; CDC immunization schedules; primary, secondary, and tertiary prevention; screenings; anticipatory guidance; health education and behavior modification.

Exam tip

Anchor the answer in what is developmentally normal versus abnormal for the client’s life stage, and in what the nurse can do to prevent or detect problems early rather than treat them later.

Major category

Psychosocial Integrity

9%
What this tests

The emotional, mental, and social dimensions of nursing care.

Key competency areas

Therapeutic communication; anxiety, depression, psychotic disorders, and substance use; grief and loss; abuse and neglect; behavioral management; end-of-life care; coping and support systems; culturally responsive care.

Exam tip

Therapeutic communication questions follow a reliable pattern: acknowledge the client’s feelings without false reassurance, advice, or judgment. Nurse-centered “I” statements and redirection are often distractors.

4
Physiological Integrity4 subcategories · 51% combined
+

This category has four subcategories and collectively represents the largest share of the exam by volume.

Subcategory

Basic Care and Comfort

9%
What this tests

Foundational physical care that supports daily living and patient dignity.

Key competency areas

Activities of daily living; nutrition and hydration; elimination; rest and sleep; mobility and positioning; pain management; assistive devices; complementary therapies.

Exam tip

When two interventions seem equally valid, choose the one that is least invasive and most supportive of the client’s independence and function.

Subcategory

Pharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

16%
What this tests

Safe medication administration and IV therapy across all routes and populations.

Key competency areas

Five rights plus three checks; dosage calculations; adverse effects and toxic reactions; high-alert medications; blood and blood products; central venous access; patient and family education; expected versus unexpected responses.

Exam tip

After medication administration, prioritize assessment of the client’s response over documentation. For adverse effects, the sequence is usually stop → assess → notify → document.

Subcategory

Reduction of Risk Potential

12%
What this tests

Monitoring and intervention to prevent complications from existing conditions or planned procedures.

Key competency areas

Lab interpretation; diagnostic test preparation and post-procedure care; hemodynamic monitoring; vital sign interpretation; system-specific complications; therapeutic procedures.

Exam tip

Critical values appear frequently. Common ranges include potassium 3.5–5.0 mEq/L and sodium 135–145 mEq/L. When an unsafe value is paired with symptoms, notify the provider rather than waiting for the next scheduled assessment.

Subcategory

Physiological Adaptation

14%
What this tests

Nursing management of acute and chronic illness, including deterioration and emergency scenarios.

Key competency areas

Pathophysiology and altered body function; fluid and electrolyte imbalances; acute versus chronic illness management; deteriorating patients; wound and drainage care; altered mental status; infectious disease management.

Exam tip

When a patient is deteriorating, use the ABC framework: airway, breathing, circulation. Prioritize the finding that threatens airway patency or oxygenation first.

How the categories interact

No NCLEX-RN question belongs entirely to one category. A single heart-failure scenario may require safety principles, pharmacological knowledge, and communication skills at the same time. The category tells you what is being emphasized, not what knowledge you are allowed to use.
