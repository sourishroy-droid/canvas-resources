The explanation is the lesson

Don’t memorize the answer. Extract the rule.

When you miss a question, memorizing that one answer will not help you, because you will never see that exact item again. What helps is finding the rule the explanation is teaching, because a rule carries over to the next question.

In this resource, you will learn a simple way to pull the rule out of any explanation and collect the most useful Care Management rules to keep beside you while you practice.

The extraction move

Pull the rule out in three steps

1
Read the whole explanation
Read why the correct option is right and why every wrong option is wrong.
2
Ask one question
What general principle made the correct answer correct and the others wrong, setting aside the specific case details?
3
Write the rule in your own words
State a principle you could apply to a completely different item.
Modeled extraction 1

Delegation

Sample item: A registered nurse is organizing care for four clients on a medical-surgical unit and is deciding which task to assign to the unlicensed assistive personnel (UAP) on the team. Which task is appropriate to delegate to the UAP?
A. Teaching a newly diagnosed client how to use an inhaler
B. Assessing a client's surgical wound at the first dressing change
C. Assisting a stable client with a bath and recording oral intake
D. Evaluating a client's response to a new pain medication
C is correct because bathing a stable client and recording intake are routine, standardized tasks that need no assessment, teaching, or clinical judgment. The other options require nursing judgment through teaching, assessment, or evaluation.
What made C right was not the bath itself. It was that the correct task needed no nursing judgment, while every wrong option did.
Rule: A task can be delegated only when it needs no nursing judgment, assessment, teaching, or evaluation. An auxiliary worker’s practical ability does not define the scope; the legal role does. This is a common adjustment for nurses trained in Mexico, where the same staff member may do more under direct supervision.
Modeled extraction 2

Confidentiality

Sample item: A client's adult sibling calls the nursing unit and asks for an update on the client's condition. The client has not given permission to share information with this sibling. How should the nurse respond?
A. Provide a brief summary since the caller is a family member
B. Explain that information cannot be shared without the client's authorization
C. Transfer the call to the physician to give the update
D. Confirm only whether the client has been admitted
B is correct because HIPAA requires the client's authorization before protected health information is disclosed, and a family relationship by itself does not grant access. Transferring the call or confirming admission does not solve the authorization problem.
The deciding detail was not the sibling or the phone call. It was that the client, not the family bond, controls who receives their information.
Rule: The client controls disclosure of their health information, and the default is non-disclosure unless the client has authorized it. Family-centered updates are common and often expected in Mexican practice, which is why this rule has to be stated explicitly.
